Three things that undermine word of mouth

Word of mouth gets someone to your website. These are what they find when they check you out.

One: the trade badge belongs to a paving company

The TrustATrader badge on your site is served from this file:

sharafroofing.co.uk · page source /wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Trustatrader-Paving-Company.png

It is a generic badge image made for a paving business. It does not link to your profile and it carries no rating or review count. A homeowner who notices reads it as decoration rather than accreditation, and TrustATrader themselves are unlikely to be pleased about it.

Two: the photographs are not your roofs

Two of the images on your site are licensed stock photography, filed as AdobeStock_235305251 and AdobeStock_387619937. Other roofers in London can and do buy the identical pictures.

The frustrating part is that you do not need them. You already have five real job photographs on the site, taken on a phone and sent over WhatsApp. Those are worth far more than the stock, because they are provably yours.

Three: a spelling mistake in a section heading

One of your service headings reads "Installing Flat Foofs". It is not buried in body copy, it is a heading. Small, but for a customer weighing up a five-figure roof it feeds the doubt they were already carrying.

Why this matters for a word-of-mouth business specifically

The mechanism

When a customer recommends you, the person they recommended you to almost always searches your name before they ring. That search is the moment the referral either converts or quietly dies.

Right now that person lands on a site with stock photos, a paving badge, a typo and no reviews. The referral did its job and the website undid it. You will never hear about the ones you lose this way, which is exactly why it goes unfixed for three years.

Fixing it does not just help advertising. It raises the conversion rate of every referral you already get, for free, permanently.

And on qualified enquiries specifically: your forms ask for a message and nothing else. If you want better-qualified leads, the fastest route is to ask better questions on the form — job type, property, postcode, timescale — so you can tell a five-figure re-roof from a gutter clean before you spend an hour on it.

The gap that costs you the bigger jobs

Foogle leads with a 10-year or 20-year insurance-backed guarantee. You say "guarantee on all work" and never state a term or who backs it.

On a full roof replacement, the guarantee is a large part of what the customer is actually buying. A specific number beats an adjective every time. If you offer ten years, say ten years.

Estimated size of the business

Estimate

Turnover roughly £250,000 to £550,000. Net profit roughly £30,000 to £70,000.

How we got there. You run a team rather than working alone — your own copy emphasises teamwork and "well trained staff" — and you cover both commercial and residential across north west London, where roof values run higher than most of the country. A full London re-roof commonly sits between £8,000 and £20,000. A small team completing roughly two to four significant jobs a month plus repairs and guttering lands in that band.

UK roofing contractors typically run 8 to 14 percent net. London labour and access costs push toward the lower half of that.

What that means. You are at the size where word of mouth alone starts to cap growth, because your referral network can only produce so many jobs a month. Everything in this audit is about making the referrals you already get convert more often before adding new channels on top.

Priority order

  1. Remove the paving badge

    Either link a real TrustATrader profile with your live rating and review count, or take the graphic down. A badge that is not yours is worse than no badge.

  2. Replace the stock photographs with your own

    You already have five real job photos. Put them front and centre and delete the Adobe Stock images. This costs nothing and is the single biggest credibility gain available.

  3. Put your reviews on your own website

    Rating, count and two or three full quotes with customer names, near the top of the page rather than nowhere at all.

  4. Fix "Foofs" and refresh the year

    Ten minutes. A live 2023 copyright tells every visitor the business may not still be trading.

  5. Rebuild the enquiry forms to qualify

    Phone, job type, property type, postcode and timescale. This is what turns enquiries into qualified enquiries.

  6. Install analytics before spending anything on marketing

    You currently cannot measure a single visit. Any money spent on advertising before this is unmeasurable by definition.
